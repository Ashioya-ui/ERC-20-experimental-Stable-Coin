# Phase 4 findings

Review of commit 522881c (2026-10-16). Severity uses the usual scale:
- **High:** direct loss of funds.
- **Medium:** loss of a core function, or of funds under plausible conditions.
- **Low:** limited impact, or impact only under unlikely conditions.
- **Info:** no security impact.

Each fix landed test-first. The failing test, then the fix, are in the commit named
`phase4(<ID>)`.

| ID | Severity | Status | Finding | Test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Medium | Fixed | The daily mint cap counted gross minting, and redemptions never freed it. A one-transaction mint-and-redeem round trip (flash-loan funded) blocked all other minting for the rest of the epoch. Measured at H = 0 with a 10,000 sUSD bootstrap cap: the round trip cost 1.98 USDC and left 1e10 wei of capacity, and a following 100 USDC mint reverted. Fix: redemptions in the epoch reduce `mintedInEpoch`, floored at 0, so the cap bounds net growth. | `test_mint_roundTripLeavesCapacityUnchanged`, `testFuzz_netGrowthPerEpochStaysCapped`, `invariant_expansionWithinCap` |
| A2 | Low | Fixed | `beforeInitialize` checked the PoolId and ignored `sqrtPriceX96`. Once the hook was deployed, anyone could initialise its one pool at any price in the next block; the deploy script's `initialize` then reverted and the pool started off-peg. Fix: the starting price must be within 50 bp of wsUSD's redemption value in the paired asset (both token orders, any decimals; new views `parPriceX96` and `priceWithinBand`). The post-deploy check asserts the band until the first recorded swap and reports a deviation after that, since trading moves the price. | `test_initialisationPriceMustBeNearPar` (red before the fix) |
| A3 | Low (stated as Medium in the prompt) | Fixed | `SpectralToken` and `AutonomousTreasury` let `deployer` call `setStabilizer` once in a later transaction, and the oracle's owner called `setHook` later too. Whoever held the deployer key between those transactions could make itself the token's sole minter or the oracle's writer. Downgraded to Low: our own `setStabilizer` / `setHook` would then have reverted and the script and post-deploy check would have failed, so a hijacked deployment could not go live unnoticed. It would only have been wasted. Fix: all wiring is constructor-fixed with addresses predicted from the deployer's nonce and the hook's mined salt. `setStabilizer`, `setHook`, the `deployer` immutables and the oracle's `Ownable` are removed; the script asserts every prediction; the stabilizer's constructor reverts with `WiringMismatch` unless the token and treasury both name it. The deployment drops from 15 to 11 transactions. | `test_stabilizerFixedAtConstruction`, `test_wiring`, `test_hookFixedAtConstruction`, `test_constructorRejectsMiswiring`, the rehearsal (11 transactions) |
| A4 | Medium | Fixed | `DEPLOYER_PRIVATE_KEY` sat in the deploy job's `env`, so every step could read it, including `forge test`, which executes dependency code: `vm.envString` plus a fork call to an attacker's URL would have exfiltrated it. Actions were pinned to floating tags (`@v4`), which a tag move can repoint. Fix: the deployer **address** is a repository variable and the key reaches only the broadcast step, which first checks that the key matches that address; every action in both workflows is pinned to a full commit SHA; `permissions: {}` at the top with per-job `contents: read`; `persist-credentials: false` on checkout; `timeout-minutes` on every job. A Slither job and an isolated-mode test run were added at the same time. | `actionlint` clean; the workflow structure itself |
| A5 | Medium (trust) | Fixed | After the 7-day timelock the admin could migrate to any contract whose `asset()` was USDC, and `migrateVault` then deposited everything above the buffer into it: about 85% of the reserve. Fix: the stabilizer takes an immutable allowlist of at most 4 vaults over the treasury's asset, which must include the vault the treasury starts with (`BadVaultAllowlist` otherwise); `proposeVault` reverts with `VaultNotAllowed` for anything else. On Base the list is Aave waBasUSDC and Gauntlet USDC Prime, and the post-deploy check asserts it. The emergency exit to idle funds never consults the allowlist. The admin role also moved to OpenZeppelin `AccessControlDefaultAdminRules`: two-step transfer with a 3-day delay, and no single-call renounce. | `test_onlyAllowlistedVaultsCanBeProposed`, `test_constructorValidatesVaultAllowlist`, `test_adminTransferIsTwoStepAndDelayed` |
| A6 | Medium (economic) | Fixed | The EMA folded once per active block with a fixed weight of 0.25, and each slot held the gross flow of its block. Measured on a real v4 PoolManager: 33 blocks (66 s) and 330 wsUSD of volume drove the smoothed H from 0.5 to 0.0375, and 17 blocks drove it to 0.962. At H = 0 the surplus buffer fell to 0.67%, paying out most of the first-loss cushion. Fix: (b) a fold carries `w = min(emaAlphaMax, dt/(dt+tau))`, tau = 1 h, so packed folds are worth ~0.00055 each; (c) slots hold the absolute **net** flow of the block, so same-block round trips and sandwiches net out; (d) D4 floors the buffer at its neutral 2.75%. After the fixes 400 consecutive blocks (13 min) move H only to 0.40. The residual is a patient attacker who paces folds: 24 hours and 810 wsUSD reaches H = 0.049, which buys a 12 bp discount on their own minting and cannot touch the buffer. Full numbers in `audit/oracle-manipulation.md`. | `test_ema_foldWeightGrowsWithTime`, `test_ema_packedFoldsAreWeakerThanSpacedFolds`, `test_netFlowPerBlockCancelsRoundTrips`, `OracleManipulationTest` (4 measurements), entropy extremes in the invariant handler |
| A7 | Low | Fixed | `MIN_AMOUNT` was a round 1 sUSD, and a holder left with less than that could not redeem at all. Fix: the minimum is derived from the bound it exists for — one wei of the reserve asset lost to vault rounding must be covered by the 1 bp minimum fee, giving `minAmount() = 10_000 * scale`, which is 0.01 sUSD for USDC, 100x lower. A holder whose whole balance is below the minimum can still exit in full (their exact balance, or the max sentinel), and a redemption that would pay out zero assets reverts. | `test_minimumIsDerivedAndDustCanStillExit` |
| A8 | Info | Fixed | `test_record_worstCaseGasFitsHookBudget` measured with `gasleft()` in the test body: 50,352 normally but 77,228 under `--isolate`, which `--gas-report` turns on, so the 70,000 budget failed there. Fix: the call is measured from inside a contract (`OracleGasMeter`), as the hook pays it, and the budget now holds in both modes. `vm.lastCallGas()` could not be used: forge-std v1.17's `Gas` struct has six fields and Foundry 1.5.1's cheatcode returns five, so decoding it reverts. Fixing this also exposed two tests that only failed under `--isolate` (a nonce prediction broken by an interleaved call, and a currency-order assumption); both are fixed, and `forge test --isolate` is now a CI step. | `test_record_worstCaseGasFitsHookBudget` in both modes; the whole suite under `--isolate` |
| A9 | Medium (economic, launch parameters) | Analysed; remedy in B8 | A holder who learns of a vault loss before it is realised can redeem at par first and shift `b · max(0, (l − U)/S − f)` onto those who stay (section 5.1 of the threat model). Measured: 248.0001 sUSD shifted for `S` = 20,000, `U` = 102, `l` = 600, `b` = 9,999, equal to the formula; a holder left after 90% of supply exits ahead of a 2% loss loses 19.81% instead of 1.99%. Losses inside the cushion lower no balance in either order. On day one the cushion is only the mint fees collected so far, so the launch parameters leave exposure; B8's protocol capital (1,250 USDC = 5% of the 25,000 sUSD cap) is the remedy. Redemption stays unpausable by design. | `testFuzz_lossWithinTheSurplusNeverLowersTheIndex`, `test_frontRunningShiftsTheExcessLossMinusTheFee`, `test_frontRunningGainsNothingInsideTheCushion`, `test_lateHoldersBearAnAmplifiedLoss` |
